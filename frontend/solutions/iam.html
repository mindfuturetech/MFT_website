FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About IAM

Quick answers about identity security, SSO, access control, zero-trust, and compliance.

What is IAM?

IAM (Identity and Access Management) is the framework of policies, processes, and technologies that ensure the right people have the right access to the right resources — and everyone else is denied. It covers authentication (who you are), authorization (what you can access), and audit (what you did).

How does Single Sign-On (SSO) improve security?

SSO lets users log in once to access all applications, eliminating password fatigue and reducing weak or reused passwords. It centralizes authentication through a single identity provider, makes access revocation instant, and pairs with MFA for a stronger security posture.

What is the difference between RBAC and ABAC?

RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) grants access based on a user's role — such as manager, finance, or admin. ABAC (Attribute-Based Access Control) grants access based on attributes like department, location, time, or device. RBAC is simpler and faster to manage; ABAC is more granular and context-aware.

How does IAM support zero-trust security?

IAM is the foundation of zero trust. Every access request is verified — regardless of whether the user is inside or outside the network. Continuous authentication, least-privilege access, MFA, and complete audit logs combine to enforce zero-trust principles across your enterprise.

Which compliance standards does IAM help with?

IAM helps organizations meet SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, and other regulatory requirements. Built-in audit logs, access reports, and user lifecycle controls provide the evidence auditors need for identity and access reviews.

Can IAM integrate with our existing directory and applications?

Yes. IAM integrates with Active Directory, LDAP, Azure AD, Google Workspace, and thousands of SaaS applications via SAML, OAuth, and SCIM. This lets you unify identity management without replacing your existing infrastructure.
